Worldview comparison across four questions: origins, meaning, morality, destiny

Ravi Zacharias's test: every worldview must pass two checks. Correspondence — does its answer match reality? Coherence — do all its answers agree with each other? Select a question to see how each system holds up.

Where did I come from?
The question every worldview must answer about the origin of the universe, life, and the individual human being.
Christianity
Created and dignified
Personal God creates intentionally. Genesis 1:27 — made in God's image.
Corresponds to fine-tuning data. Hawking: odds against our universe are enormous without a designing mind.
Passes both tests. Origin is personal and grounded in evidence of design.
Islam
Created by Allah
Intentional creation by a sovereign God. Surah 32:7-9.
Allah is solitary — no relational nature before creation. Love requires an object; 1 John 4:8 challenge applies.
Partially passes. Creation is intentional but God's pre-creation nature creates a coherence problem with love as his essence.
Buddhism
No creator, no self
No personal God. The self (anatta) is an illusion. You were not made — you arose.
No answer for why anything exists at all. The universe is beginningless — coherence problem with modern cosmology.
Fails correspondence. An eternal beginningless universe contradicts the Big Bang's evidence for a definite beginning.
Hebrew Israelites
Created as a covenant people
Intentional creation by Yahweh. Genesis 1 affirmed.
Identity restricted to a specific ethnic group. Universal human dignity in God's image becomes selective.
Affirms creation but restricts its meaning. The image of God becomes ethnic rather than universal — coherence problem with Genesis 1:27's scope.
Agnosticism / Secularism
Unknown or accidental
Naturalism: matter plus energy plus time plus chance. No designer.
Something from nothing — not physics, metaphysics. Nobel physicist: the universe is pointless.
Fails coherence. Dawkins admits natural selection cannot be trusted to produce truth — which undermines the very reasoning used to build the argument.
Why am I here?
The question of purpose — what makes any individual human life matter, and what gives it direction.
Christianity
Known and called
John 17:3 — eternal life is knowing the Father and the Son. Meaning is relational, not performative.
Jesus's own life supplies the coherence — a Person who chose significance for others over self-preservation.
Passes both tests. Meaning does not depend on achievement or feeling — it is grounded in an unchanging relationship.
Islam
To worship and obey
Surah 51:56 — humans created to worship Allah. Clear purpose statement.
Meaning contingent on performance. Assurance of acceptance is not guaranteed — Al-Ghazali acknowledged no certainty of salvation.
Corresponds to theism but coherence is strained. A meaning built on uncertain acceptance produces anxiety rather than rest.
Buddhism
To escape suffering
The First Noble Truth identifies suffering (dukkha) as the problem. The Eightfold Path is the solution.
Nirvana is the cessation of desire and self — meaningful engagement with life is the problem, not the goal.
Coherence problem: the meaning of life is to eventually have no self that experiences meaning. The system argues itself out of existence.
Hebrew Israelites
To be the chosen nation
Deuteronomy 7:6 — a holy people, chosen above all peoples. Corporate identity gives strong purpose.
Meaning is ethnically gated. Non-members have no coherent purpose within the framework — universality fails.
Fails correspondence. Acts 17:26 states God made all nations from one blood. A meaning restricted to one ethnic group contradicts the biblical data it claims to follow.
Agnosticism / Secularism
Self-constructed
Meaning is chosen, not found. Christopher Hitchens listed art, literature, and science as sufficient.
When diagnosed with terminal cancer, Hitchens wrote nothing about art giving comfort. It was absent precisely when needed most.
Fails correspondence at the point of death. Self-constructed meaning collapses when the self is threatened — the exact moment it is needed most.
How should I live?
The question every ethical system must answer — what grounds the difference between right and wrong, and why does it apply to everyone?
Christianity
Love from character
Matthew 22:37-40 — two commands summarise all ethics. Law grows from relationship, not performance.
Jesus himself models the ethic — not merely commands it. The cross is the logic of love taken to its conclusion.
Passes both tests. Moral law is grounded in God's character, not arbitrary decree — it is the shape love takes in a broken world.
Islam
Submission to divine command
Clear moral framework. Sharia provides law rooted in divine authority. Honest dealing and justice are commanded.
Divine command theory: something is good because God commands it, not because it reflects his nature. Euthyphro challenge applies — morality becomes arbitrary if grounded in will alone.
Clear commands but a coherence question: if God could command otherwise, the moral law is contingent, not absolute.
Buddhism
Non-harm, right conduct
Ahimsa (non-violence) and compassion are strong ethical contributions. The Eightfold Path is practically rich.
No personal God to ground the obligation. Why must I practise non-harm if there is no enduring self to be harmed and no lawgiver to enforce it?
Fails coherence. The ethics are admirable but float without a foundation — you cannot ground a moral obligation in a system that denies the existence of a stable moral agent.
Hebrew Israelites
Torah observance
The Torah contains one of the most sophisticated moral codes in ancient history. Rooted in divine command with personal divine witness.
Many streams apply Torah obligations selectively to one ethnic group. Outsiders carry lesser moral standing — creates a two-tier ethic that contradicts the universal image of God in Genesis 1.
Rich in content, inconsistent in scope. A moral law that applies differently by ethnicity cannot be universally binding.
Agnosticism / Secularism
Social consensus
Morality is culturally negotiated. Majority agreement, harm reduction, or evolutionary ethics are common frameworks.
Dawkins: we cannot trust our reasoning because evolution selected for survival not truth. If that is true, our moral intuitions are equally untrustworthy.
Fails both tests. A moral system that changes by census cannot claim universal obligation — and its own epistemology undermines the reasoning that builds it.
Where am I going?
The question that makes every other answer either urgent or irrelevant — what happens when this life ends?
Christianity
Resurrection and relationship
John 14:2 — a place and a Person. John 11:25 — Jesus is the resurrection, not merely its messenger.
The empty tomb is the only historically grounded claim about what happens after death. The resurrection is corroborated by hostile witnesses, multiple independent eyewitness streams, and the sociological shock it produced in first-century Judaism.
Passes both tests and uniquely provides historical evidence. Every other worldview asks you to trust a claim. Christianity asks you to examine a verifiable event.
Islam
Judgement and paradise
Clear eschatology — resurrection, judgment, paradise (Jannah) or hell (Jahannam). Surah 3:185.
No assurance before death. Al-Ghazali and Ibn Taymiyyah both acknowledged that no Muslim knows their final standing. Paradise is scale-dependent, not grace-grounded.
Corresponds to justice but coherence is strained. A destiny contingent on deeds produces no rest — and no Islamic figure has historically verified the resurrection claim with the evidentiary density the gospel provides.
Buddhism
Nirvana — extinction of self
Rebirth cycle (samsara) until nirvana — the extinction of desire and the dissolution of individual selfhood.
Coherence failure: if the self is already an illusion (anatta), who is being reborn? And who experiences nirvana if there is no experiencer?
Fails coherence. The system that says you have no self also claims to promise liberation for that non-existent self. The destination contradicts the diagnosis.
Hebrew Israelites
National restoration
Physical resurrection to a restored earth-kingdom is affirmed. Daniel 12:2 and Isaiah 26:19 are cited.
Destiny restricted to one ethnic group. The Gentiles have no redemptive future in many streams. This contradicts both Isaiah 49:6 and Revelation 7:9 which explicitly include all nations.
Uses the right texts but reaches the wrong conclusion. Isaiah 49:6 calls Israel a light to the nations — their destiny is to carry the invitation outward, not to close it inward.
Agnosticism / Secularism
Annihilation
Consciousness ends at death. The communist funeral — no hope, no mention of God, no meaning. George W. Bush described it as empty.
Justice is impossible without destiny. The Holocaust, Auschwitz, Treblinka — if time is all there is, no one answers for it. Ravi: if there is no eternity, there is no ultimate justice.
Fails both tests. Annihilation cannot produce justice, and a life lived knowing it ends in nothing cannot coherently claim that anything matters.
