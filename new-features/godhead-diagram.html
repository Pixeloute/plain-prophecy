"The question is not whether Father, Son, and Spirit are each fully divine — all serious Christian traditions agree on that. The question is: what is the nature of their unity?"

Western / Catholic / Protestant

Classical Trinity

Nicene Formula · 325 AD · Scutum Fidei

⚠ Philosophical Import

Unity = numerical identity of ousia (substance). The three are not three separate beings — they are one being with three personal distinctions. Problem: ousia and hypostasis are Greek philosophical categories not found in Scripture.

SDA · Seventh-day Adventist Framework

The Heavenly Trio

Three Co-equal, Co-eternal Beings · One in Nature & Love

✓ Biblically Textured

Unity = perfect relational, moral, and characterological oneness (echad). Three genuinely distinct persons in eternal self-giving love. Strength: the personal, social, relational language of Scripture is read at face value without Greek philosophical imports.

Anchor Texts for Each View

Deuteronomy 6:4 — The Shema

"Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is echad."

echad (אֶחָד) = composite unity, as in Gen 2:24 "one flesh." The word for absolute singularity, yachid, is never used of God in the Shema — favouring the Heavenly Trio's relational reading.

John 17:21–22 — The Prayer of Unity

"That they all may be one, as You, Father, are in Me, and I in You; that they also may be one in Us."

The unity of believers is explicitly modelled on Father-Son unity. If that unity means numerical substance-identity, believers literally enter the divine substance — which nobody claims. This strongly favours relational unity.

Isaiah 45:5 — The Monotheism Problem

"I am the LORD, and there is no other; apart from Me there is no God." (NIV)

The Classical Trinity's strongest text. ayin (אַיִן) = none, nothing. The Heavenly Trio must answer this: the three beings together constitute the one true God — there is no independent God outside this eternal community.

John 1:1 — The Word Is God

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." (ESV)

pros ton theon (with God) signals distinction; theos ēn ho logos (the Word was God) signals divine nature. Both views affirm this text — they differ only on what "God" means: one being or one divine nature shared by three.

Honest Assessment

The Heavenly Trio is more exegetically natural — it reads the personal, relational, social language of Scripture at face value, avoids importing Greek philosophical categories, and provides a more coherent account of God as love (1 John 4:8). The Classical Trinity resolves the Old Testament monotheism texts more cleanly but at the cost of categories the biblical authors never used. Neither is without tension. The Heavenly Trio's answer is more biblically textured; the Classical Trinity's answer is more philosophically tidy.
